## WAM-Policy: World Action Models with On-Policy Distillation for Quadrupedal Loco-Manipulation

- **arXiv**: [2611.24011](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24011)
- **类别**: 📌 其他（其他 / 待人工分类）
- **匹配关键词**: on-policy distillation
- **作者**: 
- **提交日期**:

### Abstract
Arm-free quadrupeds can perform loco-manipulation, walking on three legs while manipulating with a front leg, yet no existing policy for it follows language instructions. World action models (WAMs) have emerged as effective language-conditioned manipulation policies, but are pretrained for robot arms and sample slowly. WAM-Policy turns one WAM into a quadrupedal loco-manipulation policy via two stages. The first, Cross-Morphology Policy Adaptation and Acceleration, adapts the arm-centric WAM to quadrupeds and distills the transferred policy into a student with one video and two action steps by on-policy self-distillation (OPSD). This student, however, is bounded by its teacher, which only imitates demonstrations. The second stage, Task-Specific Improvement and Merging, therefore tunes the teacher by reinforcement learning (RL) and merges the slow specialists into one few-step student by multi-teacher on-policy distillation (M-OPD). On QLM-Bench, ten simulated tasks with 1,000 demonstrations synthesized without teleoperation, the transferred policy reaches 77.0%, and the OPSD student keeps 76.7% while sampling 14 times faster. Task-specific RL and M-OPD lift it to 85.0%. We also deploy the OPSD student on a real Unitree Go2 Air. WAM-Policy thus achieves a stronger quadrupedal policy at a fraction of the inference cost.

### Abstract

- **匹配关键词**: on-policy distillation
- **作者**:

### Abstract
On-policy distillation (OPD) trains a student language model to match a teacher model based on its own generations. When using OPD, one must choose whether to apply it at the sequence or token level and which divergence to minimize. However, despite the wide adoption of OPD in practice, the formal understanding of which OPD objective is appropriate for a given setting is limited. Toward addressing this gap, we theoretically analyze how the choice of OPD objective governs which tokens are prioritized, in what order, and how efficiently. Our analysis reveals that sequence-level OPD exhibits a future agreement bias, which myopic token-level objectives avoid. This bias induces a reverse learning order, in which sequence-level OPD first matches the teacher at later token positions, while at earlier positions it can favor tokens that are improbable under the teacher, and thus often undesirable. As for the choice of divergence, we prove that it strongly influences the amenability of OPD to gradient-based optimization. In particular, while the forward KL divergence enjoys a well-behaved objective landscape, other $f$-divergences, such as reverse KL, can suffer from vanishing gradients even when the student and teacher are far apart. Across language model post-training settings, we demonstrate the implications of these theoretical findings for which OPD objective is preferable, highlighting the importance of accounting for the interplay between the student, teacher, and task at hand.

### Abstract

We present Multimodal Model Multi-Teacher On-Policy Distillation Reasoner (M3OPD-Reasoner), a fully open recipe for post-training vision-language models (VLMs) over three reasoning domains: image STEM, image perception, and video. ### Abstract
In on-policy distillation (OPD), response completion, supervision delivery, and parameter updates need not share the same granularity. We develop streaming OPD by decoupling these three clocks: a response repeatedly delivers newly generated segments while generation continues, retaining the full causal prefix at each delivery and computing loss only on new positions. Quality recovery and supply-side benefits are assessed separately; end-to-end gains must account for continuation cost.

### Abstract
On-policy distillation (OPD) provides dense token-level supervision along student-generated trajectories, yet the learning benefits of longer trajectories may not scale proportionally with their computational cost. We investigate how supervision utility evolves with trajectory length. Controlled experiments reveal diminishing returns from additional supervised tokens, while showing that later positions remain learnable and exhibit substantial cross-position supervision transfer. Gradient analysis further identifies shared update components across response positions and diverse position-specific residuals whose cross-rollout consistency decreases at greater depths. Motivated by these findings, we propose Stochastic Horizons On-Policy Distillation (SH-OPD), which reduces redundant supervision by randomly varying rollout horizons while preserving access to deeper response states. SH-OPD samples a shared horizon for each training batch, enabling efficient parallel generation without per-sequence horizon scheduling overhead. Experiments on mathematical reasoning and code generation demonstrate that SH-OPD achieves slightly higher mathematical reasoning accuracy at approximately 45% of the per-update cost of standard OPD, while maintaining competitive code generation performance at around 60% of the baseline cost. These results highlight the benefits of efficient supervision allocation across response depths.

## Learning When to Continue and When to Stop: Termination-Aware OPSD

- **arXiv**: [2611.19925](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19925)
- **类别**: 📌 其他（其他 / 待人工分类）
- **匹配关键词**: OPSD
- **作者**: 
- **提交日期**:

### Abstract
On-Policy Self-Distillation (OPSD) improves language model reasoning by distilling from a frozen teacher initialized identically to the student and conditioned on privileged information. However, we identify an underexplored termination mismatch in OPSD: the student fails to reliably learn when to continue and when to stop. This mismatch manifests in two opposing failure modes-premature termination, where reasoning stops before sufficient progress is made, and repetition-driven over-generation, where the model continues generating after reasoning has stalled. Our analysis shows that the teacher-student discrepancy on EOS grows during OPSD training, while length-capped trajectories are frequently dominated by unproductive repetition. To address this issue, we propose TA-OPSD (Termination-Aware On-Policy Self-Distillation), which augments the original OPSD objective with two targeted objectives. Stop/Continue Distillation addresses premature termination by explicitly aligning the teacher and student on whether reasoning should continue, while Repetition-Aware Unlikelihood Training addresses repetition-driven over-generation by discouraging repetitive continuations in the student&#39;s failed trajectories. The resulting objective improves termination behavior from complementary directions: continuing when further reasoning is needed and stopping when reasoning becomes unproductive. Experiments on mathematical reasoning tasks with Qwen3 (1.7B/4B/8B) show that TA-OPSD consistently outperforms OPSD and other baselines across all three model scales. Beyond the performance gains, ablation and behavioral analyses show that the two proposed objectives effectively mitigate their corresponding termination failures, reducing both premature termination and repetition-driven over-generation.

In Europe, this can be achieved through implicit balancing, where a wind farm adjusts its power setpoint in anticipation of the imbalance price using information published by the Transmission System Operator. Thereby, the wind farm helps restore system balance while also reducing its own imbalance costs resulting from forecast errors. However, implicit balancing is a challenging problem since the settlement price is unknown in advance and can be impacted by the pursued control strategy.
