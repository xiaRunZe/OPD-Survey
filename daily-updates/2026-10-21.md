## Capable Teacher, Myopic Scorer: Reward-Free Teacher Repair for Agentic On-Policy Distillation

- **arXiv**: [2610.28586](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28586)
- **类别**: 🤖 Agent（Agent 应用）
- **匹配关键词**: on-policy distillation
- **作者**: 
- **提交日期**: 
- **主分类**: 

### Abstract
Agentic on-policy distillation (OPD) trains a student agent on its own trajectories while a teacher scores every token. A natural explanation of where it goes wrong is compounding error: the student&#39;s mistakes carry its trajectories beyond the region where the teacher is reliable, so recent methods keep training inside that region, through episode-length curricula, teacher-guided turns, prefix replay, or teacher intervention where the two disagree. We show this explanation is incomplete. From the states where the student departs most from the teacher, the teacher&#39;s own takeovers still finish the task, yet the student trained on its token scores keeps running out of turns. We attribute this to the myopia of next-token scoring. The teacher rates each token without seeing where the episode ends, so its scores let through the safe, delaying moves that outcome-based RL would punish with zero reward, a penalty agentic OPD lacks. We call this difference between what the teacher does and what it scores the rollout-scorer gap. We propose Rollout-to-Scorer Distillation (R2S), which repairs the scorer before distillation by mining high-disagreement states from the untrained student, running teacher takeovers from them, keeping the states where enough takeovers pass a reward-free check such as consensus or an LLM judge, and fine-tuning the teacher to give its takeover-informed scores on every assistant token from the divergence turn onward, without the takeover in context. The repaired teacher is frozen as the sole scorer for standard OPD, requiring no task reward during repair or training. Across three environments, two teachers and four students, R2S outperforms three state-of-the-art agentic-OPD methods in all twelve teacher-student-environment cells, by 12.8 success points on average over the best of them in each cell. - **类别**: 🔬 白盒OPD（白盒 OPD / 细粒度）
- **匹配关键词**: on-policy distillation
- **作者**: 
- **提交日期**: 
- **主分类**: 

### Abstract
On-policy distillation (OPD) supervises student-generated responses using sampled-token student-teacher log-ratios. A positive log-ratio penalizes a token that the student assigns higher probability to than the teacher, but its current value does not distinguish sustained high penalties from abrupt increases along a response. We examine these local changes by comparing consecutive blocks of tokens within the same response. Our diagnostic identifies penalty jumps, abrupt increases in average penalty that persist on held-out tokens. Across these jumps, penalties rise while teacher confidence decreases. We call this pattern penalty-confidence tension. Boundaries into high-penalty blocks do not exhibit comparable confidence changes, showing that local changes provide information beyond the current penalty value. Motivated by this observation, we propose Adaptive Pullback OPD (AP-OPD). At each detected jump, AP-OPD uses the preceding block as a local reference and uniformly scales all supervision coefficients in the following block so that its weighted average penalty matches the preceding block&#39;s original mean. This reference determines the scaling factor without an additional strength constant. AP-OPD improves performance on both mathematical reasoning and code generation.

- **作者**:

### Abstract
On-policy distillation (OPD) can succeed with surprisingly few training questions, motivating a view of questions as carriers of distributional knowledge transfer. How can we assess whether a newly generated question is valuable for distillation, and learn to generate better questions---without access to its correct answer? We introduce Ask-to-Distill (\method), a framework that treats synthetic questions as distribution elicitors and optimizes the teacher supervision they expose along student-generated trajectories. A student-initialized synthesizer is optimized using a distributional surrogate for question value, computed from a frozen student--teacher pair without reference answers, correctness rewards, or per-candidate student updates. The generated questions are filtered and deduplicated into a prompt-only bank for standard OPD. Signal analyses further show that optimized questions can elicit denser supervision and more total signal from shorter student responses, supporting elicited distributional supervision as an objective for synthetic distillation data. ### Abstract
Multi-teacher on-policy distillation (MOPD) integrates domain specialists into a shared student, but determining how strongly each teacher should supervise remains challenging. Existing discrepancy-based allocation can assign strong supervision even when the student already matches or exceeds the teacher&#39;s task performance, since behavioral mismatch does not necessarily imply a remaining capability deficit. We propose Capability-Aware Multi-Teacher On-Policy Distillation (CA-MOPD), which combines specialist merging with capability-guided allocation to account for what the student has already learned. CA-MOPD first merges reinforcement-learning specialists into a strong student initialization, then uses verifier-estimated teacher--student capability gaps to adjust domain-level distillation strength. Supervision decreases as capability gaps close and increases when estimated student capability regresses, while dense token-level guidance remains provided by the teachers. Across six benchmarks spanning mathematics, code generation, and instruction following, CA-MOPD achieves an average score of 32.22, outperforming naive MOPD, Open-MOPD, and the merged initialization by 0.86, 0.83, and 0.74 points, respectively. Controlled comparisons further support the benefits of merged initialization and capability-gap guidance under matched training budgets.

- **作者**:

### Abstract
Multimodal reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) provides reliable task-level supervision, yet offers limited guidance for localizing errors in visual reasoning. On-policy self-distillation (OPSD) complements this supervision with dense token-level corrections from a privileged teacher equipped with an evidence-centered visual view. However, the resulting corrections may conflate visual evidence with non-visual shifts induced by linguistic priors, response style, or the privileged view itself. We formalize this phenomenon as privileged-correction contamination: a token-level correction is contaminated when its update direction cannot be consistently attributed to the privileged visual evidence. Directly distilling contaminated corrections may therefore reinforce erroneous token-level updates. To address this issue, we propose reinforcement learning with visually attributable on-policy self-distillation, an evidence-consistent paradigm that augments task-level RLVR supervision with privileged token-level corrections grounded in visual evidence. We instantiate this paradigm with Visual-Attribution Gating (VAG), which uses the verifier-derived advantage as the optimization anchor and validates each privileged correction against the contrast between evidence-present and evidence-degraded teacher views. By admitting only corrections directionally supported by this visual contrast, VAG preserves visually grounded guidance while suppressing inconsistent residuals. Experiments across six visual reasoning benchmarks show that VAG consistently outperforms GRPO and OPSD methods, establishing new state-of-the-art average performance.

### Abstract
Multi-teacher on-policy distillation (MOPD) aggregates the capabilities of multiple domain-expert teachers into a single student model. However, all domains in MOPD share the student&#39;s finite budget of optimization steps and rollouts. Fixed uses constant domain sampling ratios based on corpus sizes; by contrast, D$^3$-MOPD adjusts the ratios using the teacher-student reverse-KL divergence and its recent decrease. However, favoring large recent decreases can direct more training toward domains that already improve quickly, while domains that improve more slowly may receive too few examples. ### Abstract

### Abstract
Unlike on-policy distillation (OPD), which depends on a separate teacher model, on-policy self-distillation (OPSD) leverages a single language model acting simultaneously as the student and the teacher, with the teacher conditioned on privileged information. Despite this architectural simplicity, synchronous rollout generation dominates overall training time, creating a severe systems bottleneck when scaling OPSD. To address this, we introduce Asynchronous On-Policy Self-Distillation (AsyncOPSD), a framework that decouples rollout generation from learner updates to improve training throughput. Across three model sizes and mathematical reasoning benchmarks, AsyncOPSD improves the training throughput by $1.96$--$2.25\times$ over synchronous OPSD while increasing average benchmark accuracy.

## Function-Space Temporal Self-Distillation for Policy Optimization

- **arXiv**: [2610.19687](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19687)
- **类别**: ♻️ OPSD（OPSD / 调度）
- **匹配关键词**: self-distillation
- **作者**: 
- **提交日期**: 
- **主分类**: 

### Abstract
Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) improves reasoning in language models, but scalar outcomes provide coarse supervision over response trajectories. Self-distillation offers denser token-level guidance by comparing an unassisted policy with a feedback-informed self-teacher, yet the teacher evolves with the student, creating unstable targets and motivating additional regularization. We introduce Function-Space Policy Optimization (FSPO), a single-policy framework that combines current privileged adaptation with function-space temporal consolidation. FSPO stores sparse historical next-token distributions as fixed temporal targets, providing temporal separation without maintaining a persistent teacher model. It further introduces privileged disagreement correction to explicitly suppress locally overvalued actions, reliability-adaptive grounding that strengthens reference supervision when self-generated evidence repeatedly fails, and quality and coverage-aware temporal memory that retains verified behaviors and improves privileged demonstrations. Across reasoning and tool-use benchmarks, FSPO achieves the highest average accuracy under a 5-hour training budget, improving over the strongest baseline by approximately 4% points on average while reducing peak memory from about 70 GB to 56 GB on Olmo3-7B-Instruct.

- **作者**:

### Abstract
Knowledge distillation (KD) improves language-model pretraining by matching teacher distributions at corpus prefixes, but provides no direct supervision at contexts induced by student predictions. We introduce PA1-OPD, which augments corpus-path distillation with teacher feedback after one student-sampled transition per selected prefix. This expands the supervised states while retaining next-token prediction and corpus-path KD. Data-anchored roots support parallel sampling, and differentiable tree attention shares prefix computation without changing inference. In two continued-pretraining settings, full coverage improves held-out cross-entropy over KD across C4, PG-19, and Pile at every evaluated checkpoint. In Stage2, selecting the 10% of roots with highest student surprisal adds 48.85% to KD&#39;s Macro cross-entropy gain over next-token training. Gains remain when branch supervision excludes successor states already on the corpus path, supporting the value of feedback beyond observed contexts. Additional depth or breadth improves aggregate results at unchanged per-term supervision weight, with benefits varying by corpus. These findings support local student-induced supervision as a complement to pretraining distillation.

## Divergence-Routed On-Policy Distillation for Language Models

- **arXiv**: [2610.19572](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19572)
- **类别**: ♻️ OPSD（OPSD / 理论）
- **匹配关键词**: on-policy distillation
- **作者**: 
- **提交日期**: 
- **主分类**: 

### Abstract
On-policy distillation (OPD) transfers knowledge from a teacher model to a student along student-generated trajectories, typically by minimizing the reverse Kullback-Leibler (RKL) divergence. However, RKL can provide weak corrective signals for teacher-supported tokens to which the student assigns very low probability. This raises two questions: which positions are most consequential for the behavioral gap between the student and teacher, and what additional supervision is needed at these positions? Through cross-sampling, we find that divergence-based routing more effectively pinpoints positions contributing to the teacher-student performance gap than entropy-based routing. Further analysis reveals teacher-mode under-coverage at high Jensen-Shannon (JS) divergence positions, where standard OPD increases the large teacher-student probability gaps. Motivated by these findings, we propose Divergence-Routed On-Policy Distillation (DrOPD). DrOPD retains RKL supervision at all positions and selectively adds forward Kullback-Leibler (FKL) divergence supervision at positions with high JS divergence. Experiments across six math benchmarks show that DrOPD improves over standard OPD and entropy-based routing. DrOPD also reduces severe under-coverage cases by 37.9 percent relative to OPD, supporting targeted FKL as an effective complement to OPD.

## Demystifying On-Policy Distillation: Where Does the Teacher&#39;s Advantage Live?

- **类别**: 📌 其他（其他 / 待人工分类）

- **作者**: 
- **提交日期**:

### Abstract
On-policy distillation (OPD) is increasingly studied as a cheaper alternative to reinforcement learning, yet it remains a form of knowledge distillation (KD) that relies on teacher supervision. We revisit OPD from the KD perspective by asking where the teacher&#39;s advantage lies along student-generated trajectories. Controlled token flips at teacher-student disagreements directly measure the accuracy gained from teacher corrections. Single-token flips yield a modest average gain on selected failed trajectories, whereas repeated flips raise accuracy from 28.4% to 74.5%, close to the teacher&#39;s 76.9%. Gains continue to accumulate with more flips, but mass- and KL-based selection outperform random flips by 15-18 percentage points at counts of similar magnitude. Selective intervention remains useful after OPD, but greater argmax agreement does not consistently improve accuracy across model pairs. We advocate training schedules that learn more valuable teacher corrections while maintaining distributional stability. Controlled flips and fork-resolution analysis evaluate OPD beyond accuracy by measuring which teacher preferences the student acquires and what further correction can recover.
